================================================================================
PART SUBSTITUTION PIPELINE -- SCHEMA AND DESIGN DECISIONS
================================================================================

Shared contract between the data pipeline (Python, outside Foundry) and the
Foundry application. Neither side changes anything here unilaterally.

Each section gives the structure, then WHY it is that way. The rationale
matters more than the columns -- most of these decisions were made after
something broke, and the reasoning is what stops it being undone later.


================================================================================
1. GOVERNMENT LAYER (what the part must be)
================================================================================

  federal_items                          [PK: nsn]
      nsn, fsc, item_name, governing_spec_ref, cage_code
        |
        |--< requirement_profiles        [PK: requirement_id]
        |       requirement_id, nsn(FK), requirement_name, operator,
        |       value, uom, requirement_class, source, source_spec_id(FK),
        |       conflicting_value, conflicting_source
        |
        |--< price_history               [PK: price_id]
        |       price_id, nsn(FK), fiscal_year, unit_price_avg,
        |       quantity, vendor_cage, contract_count
        |
        >--- specifications              [PK: spec_id]
                spec_id, title, requirement_count
                  |
                  |--< spec_requirements [PK: spec_requirement_id]
                  |       spec_requirement_id, spec_id(FK), requirement_name,
                  |       operator, value, uom, requirement_class,
                  |       confidence, source_page
                  |
                  |--< requirement_profiles.source_spec_id

--- WHY THREE TABLES FOR REQUIREMENTS ---

Requirements arrive from two different places and have to end up in one.

  PUB LOG characteristics  -- structured, reliable, often sparse
  MIL-SPEC extraction      -- rich, unstructured, LLM-derived, fallible

requirement_profiles is the MERGED view, one row per requirement per NSN.
This is the only table the application queries.

--- MERGE PRECEDENCE, WITH A CONFIDENCE FLOOR ---

Where both sources cover the same attribute:

    spec extraction confidence >= 0.70   --> specification wins
    spec extraction confidence <  0.70   --> PUB LOG wins; the extraction
                                             is retained in spec_requirements
                                             for provenance only

Without a floor, an LLM misreading a table in a MIL-SPEC PDF silently beats
reliable structured data, and the resulting row looks identical to a good
one. The precedence rule is right -- specifications ARE more authoritative
than catalog characteristics -- but only when the extraction is trustworthy.

--- CONFLICTS ARE STORED, NOT LOGGED ---

When the two sources disagree, the losing value goes in conflicting_value
and its origin in conflicting_source. Both nullable.

Logging to stdout means the disagreement vanishes. Storing it makes source
disagreement a QUERYABLE FINDING -- "PUB LOG and MIL-DTL-38999 disagree on
operating temperature for this NSN" is genuinely interesting output, not
diagnostics.

specifications and spec_requirements exist for PROVENANCE ONLY. They carry
confidence and source_page so an extracted requirement can be checked
against the original PDF. An LLM-extracted requirement nobody can audit is
worse than a missing one.

This split also makes S3/S4 cuttable. If spec extraction fails or runs out
of time, requirement_profiles still works -- every row just has
source = publog_characteristics and a null source_spec_id.

--- WHY federal_items IS THE HUB ---

Requirements, prices, candidates, and slots all key off nsn. If S1 fails,
nothing downstream exists. That is why it is step 1 in the build order and
why it gets tested against 5 hand-picked NSNs before anything else is built.


================================================================================
2. COMMERCIAL LAYER (what you can buy)
================================================================================

  commercial_parts                       [PK: mpn]
      mpn, manufacturer, lifecycle_status, median_price,
      stock_qty, lead_time_days, datasheet_url, distributor_count

--- WHY lifecycle_status AND distributor_count MATTER MOST ---

These two fields are the reason the Nexar/Octopart quota gets spent at all.
Digi-Key gives parametric search for free; it does not give cross-distributor
lifecycle or multi-source availability.

An obsolete or single-distributor candidate is a bad substitution regardless
of price. Obsolescence and single-sourcing are the substantive findings.
Price is secondary.


================================================================================
3. MATCHING LAYER (the join -- this is the product)
================================================================================

  substitution_candidates                [PK: candidate_id]
      candidate_id, nsn(FK), candidate_mpn(FK), risk_level, risk_rank,
      pass_count, fail_count, marginal_count, unknown_count,
      qpl_listed, gov_unit_price, gov_quantity, commercial_unit_price,
      commercial_basis_qty, price_delta_indicative, rationale
        |
        |  nsn            --> federal_items.nsn
        |  candidate_mpn  --> commercial_parts.mpn
        |
        |--< spec_deltas                 [PK: delta_id]
                delta_id, candidate_id(FK), nsn, candidate_mpn,
                requirement_name, required_value, candidate_value, verdict

--- WHY candidate_id IS A SURROGATE KEY ---

  Format: {nsn}__{candidate_mpn}

The natural key is composite (nsn + candidate_mpn). Foundry object types
accept only a SINGLE primary key property. A composite makes the
substitution_candidates -> spec_deltas link unreliable.

nsn and candidate_mpn remain as ordinary properties. Nothing is lost.

--- WHY THERE IS NO "estimated_savings" FIELD ---

    gov_unit_price          contract unit price, most recent fiscal year
    gov_quantity            quantity on that contract
    commercial_unit_price   distributor median price
    commercial_basis_qty    quantity break that price is quoted at
    price_delta_indicative  gov_unit_price - commercial_unit_price

These two prices ARE NOT COMPARABLE. The government figure is a contract
unit price carrying qualification, lot traceability, low-quantity buys, and
contract overhead. The commercial figure is typically a volume break at
around 1000 units.

Subtracting one from the other does not measure savings. It mostly measures
QUANTITY and QUALIFICATION.

So the field is named price_delta_indicative, not estimated_savings, and
both source prices plus both quantity bases are carried alongside it. The UI
must display all four -- never the delta alone.

A procurement officer will check this number first. It is the single easiest
claim in the whole project to falsify, and calling a quantity effect a
saving is exactly the naive framing this tool exists to argue against.

If time allows, normalize both to a stated quantity basis and say which.
If not, the honest column names and side-by-side display are sufficient.

Null when either input is missing. NEVER default to 0 -- the UI must
distinguish "no data" from "no difference". If every approved candidate has
a null delta, display "Unavailable", not "$0".

--- WHY THE DELTA IS COMPUTED UPSTREAM ---

So the candidate table can sort on it SERVER-SIDE. A function-backed derived
column cannot be reliably sorted on in Workshop.

Cost: S10 (price history) must run BEFORE S9's final pass. S10 costs no API
quota, so this is a cheap reorder.

--- HOW qpl_listed IS DERIVED ---

    Join qpl on (qualified_part_number AND cage_code)
    -- or (qualified_part_number AND manufacturer_name) where CAGE is absent
    -- filtered to qpl.governing_spec = federal_items.governing_spec_ref

The PAIR is what makes the join safe. Matching on qualified_part_number
alone is forbidden (see section 6) because MPNs are not unique across
manufacturers, and a false qualification match is the most dangerous error
this tool can produce.

Written down explicitly because section 6 forbids the obvious mechanism
without naming the replacement, and someone will otherwise reimplement the
dangerous version.

qpl_listed gates risk_level = low. It is the highest-consequence boolean in
the schema. If the join cannot be made confidently, emit false -- never
null-coerced-to-true.

--- WHY THIS TABLE IS THE PRODUCT ---

It is the only table joining the government and commercial worlds. Everything
else is either input to it or explanation of it.

The output is never "here is a cheaper part". It is "this candidate meets
9 of 11 requirements, fails operating temperature, and is not QPL-listed
against the governing specification."

That framing matters: MIL-SPEC parts are expensive because of qualification
(temperature range, vibration, lot traceability, QPL status), not vendor
markup. A tool that only shows price deltas is making a claim a procurement
officer knows is wrong.

--- SORT PRECEDENCE ENFORCES THAT ARGUMENT ---

    PRIMARY:    risk_rank ascending
    SECONDARY:  price_delta_indicative descending, WITHIN a risk band only

Price is never the primary sort and is never sortable across risk bands.

Whatever is sortable becomes the default sort, and the default sort IS the
argument the interface is making. Materializing a price delta and then
letting it lead would undercut the entire pitch. Risk leads; price breaks
ties.


================================================================================
4. ASSEMBLY LAYER (where parts sit)
================================================================================

  assemblies                             [PK: assembly_id]
      assembly_id, name, parent_assembly_id (self-referencing)
        |
        |--< slots                       [PK: slot_id]
                slot_id, assembly_id(FK), slot_name, baseline_nsn(FK)
                  |
                  |  baseline_nsn --> federal_items.nsn
                  |
                  |--< interfaces        [PK: interface_id]
                          interface_id, slot_a(FK), slot_b(FK),
                          constrained_attribute, match_rule

--- WHY SLOTS INSTEAD OF SUBSTITUTING PARTS DIRECTLY ---

A Slot is a POSITION in the assembly. Substitution replaces what fills the
slot; it never mutates the assembly.

If you overwrite the part in the assembly you lose the baseline, and with it
the ability to audit, revert, or compare. With slots:

  current part = the approved substitution for this slot in the active
                 configuration, else the slot's baseline_nsn

That one rule gives baseline-vs-modified comparison for free.

--- WHY assembly STRUCTURE IS SYNTHETIC ---

Real weapon system bills of material run into ITAR. Assembly names must be
generic (power_distribution, signal_conditioning). Catalog data is real;
assembly structure is invented.

State this in the UI. It is a strength -- it shows the data governance was
thought about -- not a caveat to bury.

--- WHY INTERFACE COMPARISON IS DIRECTIONAL ---

  slot_a = LEFT operand, slot_b = RIGHT operand

      numeric_gte           value(a) >= value(b)
      numeric_lte           value(a) <= value(b)
      numeric_equal         value(a) == value(b)
      exact                 string equality
      numeric_within_pct:N  abs(a-b) / abs(b) * 100 <= N

gte, lte, and within_pct are all asymmetric. Without a stated convention the
function author has to guess which side is which, and half the constraints
would evaluate backwards while still returning plausible-looking results.

Edge cases:
      parse failure / missing / non-finite  --> severity 'unverified'
      within_pct where b == 0               --> pass only if a == 0

constrained_attribute must EXACTLY match a requirement_name present in
requirement_profiles. S11 validates this and fails loudly -- a dangling
attribute reference produces silent non-checking, which looks like the
system working.

--- WHY CONFLICTS ARE REPORTED, NEVER RESOLVED ---

Compatibility is order-dependent: substituting A then B can succeed where
the reverse fails. That is real engineering behaviour, but in a UI it looks
like a bug when the user backtracks.

Solution: re-evaluate EVERY interface across the whole configuration after
each decision, and surface violations as a live list. A conflicting choice
is flagged, not blocked. The user decides.

Do NOT implement constraint solving or auto-optimization. That is a genuine
CSP and it will consume the entire build.


================================================================================
5. DECISION LAYER (empty at load, written by Foundry actions)
================================================================================

  configurations                         [PK: config_id]
      config_id, name, created_by, created_at
        |
        |--< substitution_decisions      [PK: decision_id]
                decision_id, config_id(FK), slot_id(FK), candidate_id(FK),
                chosen_mpn, verdict, is_current, justification,
                decided_by, decided_at, superseded_at
                  |
                  |  slot_id      --> slots.slot_id
                  |  candidate_id --> substitution_candidates.candidate_id

  decision_id is deterministic: {config_id}__{slot_id}__{candidate_id}

--- STATE RULES ---

      - Active approval:  verdict = approved AND is_current = true
      - At most ONE active approval per (config_id, slot_id)
      - Approving supersedes the prior approval atomically:
            is_current = false, superseded_at stamped
      - Rejections and flags always have is_current = false
      - Revert sets is_current = false
      - NOTHING IS EVER DELETED

--- WHY is_current INSTEAD OF DELETING ---

Deleting a superseded approval destroys its justification, and the
justification is the entire point of the audit trail. But leaving two
approvals active makes "which part is currently in this slot?"
nondeterministic.

is_current resolves both: deterministic current state AND full decision
history. Costs two columns.

--- WHY justification IS REQUIRED WITH NO DEFAULT ---

It is what makes the output an audit trail rather than a toy. An approval
with no stated reason is not a procurement decision, it is a click.

--- WHY THE ACTION TAKES A CANDIDATE OBJECT, NOT AN MPN ---

A free-form MPN parameter lets a caller submit a valid part number that
belongs to a DIFFERENT federal item. Passing the SubstitutionCandidate
object makes that impossible by construction.

If a free-form parameter is unavoidable, validate that the candidate exists,
that candidate_mpn matches, and that nsn equals slot.baseline_nsn.


================================================================================
6. UNLINKED (reference only)
================================================================================

  qpl                                    [PK: qpl_id]
      qpl_id, governing_spec, manufacturer_name, cage_code,
      qualified_part_number, qualification_date

--- WHY qpl IS NOT JOINED ---

qpl_listed is already materialized as a boolean on substitution_candidates,
so the application never needs to traverse to it.

DO NOT link commercial_parts.mpn to qpl.qualified_part_number.
Manufacturer part numbers are not guaranteed unique across manufacturers,
so that join can produce false qualification matches -- the most dangerous
possible error in this tool.

7. PIPELINE-ONLY (never reach Foundry)
================================================================================

  mcrl              nsn, reference_part_number, cage_code
  characteristics   nsn, attribute_name, attribute_value, uom

S1 outputs, consumed by S5, then done. mcrl is the NSN <-> manufacturer part
number cross-reference -- the join key that makes the whole project work, but
only during ingestion.


================================================================================
8. ENUMS
================================================================================

  requirement_class   electrical | mechanical | environmental |
                      qualification | traceability

  source              publog_characteristics | spec_extraction |
                      manual_override

  operator            eq | gte | lte | range | in_set | boolean

      Appears on requirement_profiles and spec_requirements. CLOSED SET,
      for the same reason match_rule is closed -- an open enum is a contract
      violation waiting to happen, one table over.

      DELIBERATELY SEPARATE from match_rule. operator describes how a
      requirement constrains a single part ("operating temp gte -55C").
      match_rule describes how two parts must agree with each other
      ("both sides within 5%"). Different jobs, different vocabularies.
      Do not merge them.

      'range' uses value formatted "min|max". 'in_set' uses pipe-delimited
      allowed values. Both are pre-normalized like everything else.

  lifecycle_status    active | nrnd | obsolete | unknown

  risk_level          low | medium | high
  risk_rank           1 | 2 | 3

      *** SORT ON risk_rank, NEVER ON risk_level ***

      Alphabetical ordering of the strings gives:  high -> low -> medium

      That is not obviously wrong on screen. It silently presents the
      WORST candidates first while looking like it sorted correctly.
      This is why the integer exists.

  verdict (spec_deltas)
                      pass | fail | marginal | unknown

      *** 'unknown' IS NEVER TREATED AS 'pass' ***

      An unverifiable requirement is a risk, not a success. It must use
      warning semantics and be visually distinct from pass in the UI.
      A high proportion of unknowns is itself a finding.

  verdict (substitution_decisions)
                      approved | rejected | flagged_for_test

  match_rule          exact | numeric_equal | numeric_gte |
                      numeric_lte | numeric_within_pct:N

      Closed set. The pipeline emits only these; Foundry implements only
      these. Anything else is a contract violation, not a feature request.


================================================================================
9. NORMALIZATION CONTRACT
================================================================================

All values in requirement_profiles.value and spec_deltas.candidate_value are
PRE-NORMALIZED to a single unit per attribute by the pipeline.

Foundry performs NO unit conversion.

--- WHY ---

Two places doing normalization means two places that can disagree, and no
way to tell which produced a wrong number. Unit mismatch does not raise an
error -- it returns a confident wrong answer.

If a value looks wrong in Foundry, fix it upstream and re-upload. Do not
patch it in a Pipeline Builder transform.


================================================================================
10. RISK SCORING
================================================================================

Per requirement, each candidate gets one of: pass | fail | marginal | unknown

Rolled up:

  high    -- any qualification-class requirement fails,
             OR more than 2 requirements fail
  medium  -- 1-2 non-qualification requirements fail,
             OR more than 30% unknown,
             OR more than 30% marginal
  low     -- all requirements pass or marginal, AND QPL-listed,
             AND marginal count is at most 30%

--- WHY marginal IS CAPPED TOO ---

Without a cap, a candidate with eleven marginals and zero fails scores low.
Eleven attributes that each nearly miss is not a low-risk substitution; it
is a part that is wrong in eleven small ways. Same reasoning as the unknown
cap -- accumulated near-misses are a finding, not a pass.

--- WHY qualification-CLASS FAILURES ARE AUTOMATIC HIGH ---

Electrical and mechanical specs are negotiable with testing. Qualification
and traceability requirements (QPL status, lot traceability, DFARS
counterfeit rules) are not. A part that fails those cannot be substituted
regardless of how well it matches on paper.

--- WHY UNKNOWNS DRIVE RISK UPWARD ---

A candidate with 10 passes and 5 unknowns is not better than one with 12
passes and 3 fails. It is less understood. Treating absence of evidence as
a pass is the single most dangerous failure mode in this tool.

12. DATA PROVENANCE (must be surfaced in the UI)
================================================================================

  Federal catalog, characteristics, cross-references .... DLA PUB LOG
  Qualification data ................................... DLA QPL
  Specification requirements ........................... ASSIST (MIL-SPEC)
  Commercial part data ................................. Digi-Key, Octopart
  Historical government pricing ........................ USAspending.gov
  Assembly structure ................................... SYNTHETIC

Nothing was scraped from a source that prohibits it. This is worth being
able to say out loud.

--- STATED SCOPE BOUNDARY: EXPORT CONTROL ---

This tool does NOT model export control classification. There is no
requirement_class for it and no field on substitution_candidates. A
substitution scored low risk here has not been assessed for ITAR or EAR
implications.

This is a deliberate cut, not an oversight. The synthetic assembly structure
handles the larger ITAR exposure -- no real bill of material is represented
-- but export classification of individual parts is out of scope.

State this in the UI alongside the provenance table. A named boundary reads
as judgment; a silent gap reads as a miss.
